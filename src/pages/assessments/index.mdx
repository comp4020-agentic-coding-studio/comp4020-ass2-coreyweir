You choose a target in week 2 and keep it for the whole semester. The three
assignments are the same problem at three stages, and each one is marked on
your reasoning rather than on whether the thing runs.

**Choose that target carefully.** Something trivial leaves you with no
trade-offs to analyse and very little to write about. Something enormous leaves
you unable to measure anything. You also choose your own new requirement in the
final assignment, so pick something with room to grow.

The last assignment asks you to mark your own first one. That is not a
formality: calibration — knowing when you became wrong, and what evidence
should have caught it — is the single largest criterion in the course.
