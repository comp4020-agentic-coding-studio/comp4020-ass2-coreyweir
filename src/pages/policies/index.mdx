# Policies and support

## Extensions

Ask before the deadline and you will get one. Ask after it and the answer
depends on circumstances you will have to explain, which is worse for everyone.

Extensions are granted on the assignment, not on the semester: your target
carries across all three, so a late Assignment 1 shortens the time you have to
measure things for Assignment 2. If you are far enough behind that this
matters, say so early and we will re-scope your target rather than extend you
into a corner.

## Coding agents

**Use them. Say so.**

This course assumes you will have an agent write most of your proofs of
concept, because that is what the work looks like now and pretending otherwise
would make the assessment dishonest rather than rigorous. Building a prototype
is the cheap half. What is marked is which prototypes you decided were worth
building, what you measured, and whether your conclusion follows from your
evidence — none of which an agent can do for you, and all of which it will
happily appear to do.

Two rules, both practical rather than moral:

1. **Declare it.** A sentence naming what you used and for what. Nobody loses
   marks for the answer.
2. **You are responsible for every claim you submit.** A number an agent
   produced and you did not check is a number you made up. This is a course
   about not trusting measurements from hosts that are more permissive than
   your target; the same scepticism applies to the tool that generated them.

## Academic integrity

The usual rules apply and are not interesting. The one worth stating plainly:
your target, your reduction, your measurements and your audit are yours.
Discussing approaches with other students is encouraged — the sessions are
built on it — and submitting somebody else's reasoning as your own is not.

If two submissions reach the same conclusion by the same route, that is
normally fine. If they reach it by the same *mistakes*, we will ask.

## Getting help

Ask in the session. That is what it is for, and the person next to you has
probably hit the same wall from the other direction.

Between sessions, email works. Include what you tried, what happened, and what
you expected instead — not because it is a rule, but because the three of them
together usually contain the answer.

If something outside the course is making it hard to keep up, tell us early.
We can adjust a target, a deadline or a scope. We cannot do any of that in the
last week.

## Accessibility

If any part of this course is inaccessible to you — the sessions, the tooling,
the way material is delivered — tell us and we will change it. Course material
is plain text under version control precisely so that it can be reformatted,
re-rendered or read aloud without asking anybody's permission.
